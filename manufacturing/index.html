RICE-MFGManufacturing EditionIn development

One ecosystem. Honest about the starting line.

Same 9 pillars, same maturity model, same citation discipline as RICE-FSI — but built on a deliberately honest premise: most Malaysian manufacturers invest in security only when production stops, a customer contract demands it, or a fine forces the issue. RICE-MFG designs for that reality instead of pretending it away.

§ 00 · WHY A SEPARATE EDITION

Same architecture. A different starting line.

FSI and Healthcare frameworks assume a baseline of regulatory pressure already pushing maturity upward. Manufacturing, in Malaysia specifically, largely doesn't have that yet — so RICE-MFG starts from where plants actually are.

01

ROI-gated, not risk-gated

Security investment gets approved when it protects revenue, not when it reduces abstract risk — the same adoption pattern seen globally with AI investment. Framing objectives around "reduce risk" alone doesn't move budget on the plant floor; framing them around protecting the thing that already has budget authority does.

02

Production continuity is the real risk appetite

The line staying up is the one thing that reliably gets attention. Ransomware is the sharpest threat precisely because it's the one attack type that guarantees downtime — RICE-MFG treats that as the practical entry point for every other control.

03

OT/IT convergence, not generic IT security

A compromised infusion pump and a compromised PLC fail differently. RICE-MFG benchmarks specifically against IEC 62443's zones-and-conduits model rather than reusing another sector's device-security framing.

04

Fewer people on the floor means more eyes on the network

Smart manufacturing and lights-out production don't just automate labour — they remove the human who used to notice something wrong. An unmonitored autonomous line fails silently. RICE-MFG treats rising autonomy as a reason to raise visibility and contingency planning, not lower them.

§ 01 · BENCHMARK SET

What RICE-MFG is built against.

A global OT/ICS standard, layered with the honest Malaysian adoption pattern global frameworks don't account for.

IEC 62443

The core OT/ICS security standard — zones and conduits, security levels, asset-owner and integrator responsibilities. The closest existing analogue to what RICE-MFG needs to encode.

Cybersecurity Act 2024 (Act 854)

Manufacturing isn't its own NCII sector, but overlaps Trade, Industry & Economy — and any plant supplying an NCII sector inherits obligations indirectly through its customer's supply-chain requirements.

Purdue Model — Level 3.5 (Industrial DMZ)

The formal buffer zone between OT (Levels 0–3) and IT (Levels 4–5) — and the exact boundary where responsibility ownership gets ambiguous in most plants. RICE-MFG treats this handshake as a governance objective, not just a network diagram detail.

Ransomware-as-forcing-function

Not a formal standard — a documented adoption reality. RICE-MFG treats ransomware readiness as the practical entry point that earns budget for everything else.

Cyber-insurance & customer contracts

Increasingly the real governance forcing-function — insurers and OEM customers now mandate baseline controls that regulation alone hasn't required.

§ 02 · STATUS

All 9 pillars drafted. None yet validated.

Every pillar carries a full objective set, benchmarked against IEC 62443 and calibrated to a realistic starting maturity. Every objective is marked DRAFT until it's been pressure-tested against real manufacturing-sector engagement — that validation happens as exposure grows, not before.
