USD 81M stolen via fraudulent SWIFT MT103 messages. Attackers bypassed multi-layer controls using insider knowledge of SWIFT messaging flows.

18,000+ organisations compromised via trojanised Orion update. Demonstrated that trusted vendor software is an attack vector — relevant to every FSI with SolarWinds or similar tools.

§ 02 · FRAMEWORK ANALYSIS

Five frameworks. Five perspectives.

Each framework was built for a purpose. Understanding that purpose reveals where each one stops.

BNM RMiTThe Malaysian standard — mandatory for all licensed FIs · Malaysia, 2020
Strengths
  • Covers technology risk management, cybersecurity, cloud, outsourcing, and incident reporting
  • Section 11 addresses cybersecurity controls: governance, asset management, access control, incident management
  • BNM enforces with audit powers — institutions must submit Technology Risk Assessments annually
  • 2024 updates added guidance on AI risk, cloud security, and third-party risk management
Limitations & Gaps
  • Limited prescriptive technical controls — what to achieve, not how
  • Minimal AI/quantum threat specifics beyond policy guidance
  • Does not address OT/ICS or physical-cyber convergence
  • Compliance cycle risk: 'audit-ready' ≠ 'operationally resilient'
NIST CSF 2.0The global risk-based language of cybersecurity · USA, 2024 (v2.0)
Strengths
  • Six functions: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover — v2.0 added 'Govern'
  • Tiered maturity model (Tier 1 Partial → Tier 4 Adaptive) maps well to organisational readiness
  • Technology-agnostic: works across IT, OT, cloud, and hybrid environments
  • Widely used in Malaysian FSI as a complement to BNM RMiT — bridges to global frameworks
Limitations & Gaps
  • Voluntary and self-assessed — no external accountability unless paired with audit
  • Broad categories require significant interpretation for FSI operational context
  • v2.0 supply chain guidance still maturing
  • No Malaysia-specific regulatory alignment built in
CISACritical infrastructure guidance from the US cyber defence agency · USA, ongoing
Strengths
  • Zero Trust Maturity Model (ZTMM): 5 pillars — Identity, Devices, Networks, Applications, Data
  • Strong guidance on OT/ICS security (ICS-CERT advisories, Purdue model alignment)
  • Supply chain risk management (C-SCRM) framework with vendor assessment guidance
  • Actionable advisories on specific CVEs, threat actors, and attack patterns
Limitations & Gaps
  • US-centric — no direct BNM/Malaysia regulatory mapping
  • Advisory only — no certification or audit mechanism
  • OT guidance assumes US critical infrastructure context; FSI OT (ATMs, payment terminals) differs
  • Requires significant adaptation for Malaysian institutional context
OWASPThe gold standard — for the application layer only · Global, ongoing
Strengths
  • OWASP Top 10 (2021): injection, broken auth, SSRF, security misconfiguration — the AppSec bible
  • ASVS (Application Security Verification Standard): levels 1–3 for different risk tiers
  • SAMM (Software Assurance Maturity Model): measure and improve SDLC security posture
  • API Security Top 10: increasingly critical as Malaysian FSI adopts Open Banking & APIs
Limitations & Gaps
  • Scope is exclusively application/API layer — nothing on network, identity, infrastructure, cloud
  • No regulatory framework mapping or compliance alignment
  • Does not address supply chain, OT, insider threats, or AI risks
  • Requires developer/AppSec expertise to implement correctly — not C-suite accessible
ISO 27001The international ISMS standard · Global, 2022 revision
Strengths
  • 93 controls across 4 themes: Organisational, People, Physical, Technological (2022 revision)
  • New controls added: threat intelligence, cloud security, data masking, secure coding, monitoring
  • Internationally recognised certification — valued for third-party assurance and procurement
  • Risk-based approach: organisations select controls based on risk assessment
Limitations & Gaps
  • Control selection is subjective — scope can be gamed to achieve certification without real coverage
  • Management framework only — does not prescribe implementation depth or technical specifics
  • 2–3-year audit cycles may miss rapidly evolving threats
  • No explicit FSI operational requirements or BNM RMiT crosswalk
§ 03 · THE CONSULTING TRUTH

Why RICE exists.

The Compliance Treadmill

FSI institutions run an endless cycle of audits, policies, and framework adoptions. The result is an organisation that is 'audit-ready' but not necessarily operationally resilient. Compliance is a baseline — not a destination.

The C-Suite Rotation Problem

Every 2–3 years, new leadership brings new priorities and new frameworks to champion. Institutional knowledge resets. What should be a maturing security posture becomes a perpetual restart.

No Single Vendor Covers Everything

Every vendor has a product line. Their advice — however well-intentioned — is bounded by what they carry. A true consulting engagement looks at the full picture: what is happening, what you need, and the best tool for that job — regardless of vendor.

The Gap That Needed Naming

After years of seeing the same blind spots appear across FSI clients — OT, AI threats, quantum, vendor-agnostic controls — it became clear: the gap needed a framework. RICE is that framework.
