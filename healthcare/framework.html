P1DRAFT

Governance & Risk Oversight

CLINICAL-RISK-WEIGHTED · BOARD ACCOUNTABILITY · PATIENT SAFETY

The foundation pillar — but in healthcare, risk appetite has to be weighed against patient safety, not just financial exposure. Draft v0.1, benchmarked against HIPAA, HITRUST, and the Malaysian PDPA/APHM gap.

Establish a Health Information Security Governance framework with joint clinical-leadership and board accountability

HIPAA §164.308(a)(1)HITRUST 00.a◆ RICE-HC

Joint clinical + IT governance sign-off — existing frameworks assign accountability to IT/compliance alone, not clinical leadership

Define risk appetite weighted by patient-safety impact, not financial exposure alone

HIPAA §164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)HITRUST 03.a◆ RICE-HC

Patient-safety-weighted risk scoring — no existing framework ties cyber risk appetite to clinical harm severity

Implement continuous compliance monitoring beyond the annual HIPAA risk analysis cycle

HIPAA §164.308(a)(8)HITRUST 03.b

Mandate cybersecurity and patient-data-handling literacy training for clinical and administrative staff annually

HIPAA §164.308(a)(5)HITRUST 01.b

Establish a Digital Health Risk Committee with escalation to Medical Director and Board

HITRUST 00.a◆ RICE-HC

Bridges clinical and IT governance — a structural gap HITRUST references but doesn't mandate

Align RICE-HC maturity model scoring to board and clinical-governance reporting — Levels 1–5 per pillar

◆ RICE-HC

RICE Maturity Model, carried over unchanged from RICE-FSI — the one element that stays identical across every sector

Map technology risk to regulatory obligations: PDPA sensitive-data provisions, APHM code of practice, and pending Malaysian Cybersecurity Bill requirements

PDPA (sensitive data)◆ RICE-HC

Interim gap-fill mapping — Malaysia currently has no dedicated digital-health security law; MOH's own digital health lead has confirmed the absence of a mandated standard

Address clinical and IT leadership rotation risk — maintain institutional security memory across Medical Director / CISO transitions

◆ RICE-HC

Rotation-resilient governance, adapted from RICE-FSI P1.08 — healthcare leadership turnover carries the same institutional-memory risk

Key Controls (draft)

  • Board- and Medical-Director-approved Digital Health Security Strategy, reviewed annually
  • Clinical Risk Register — patient-safety impact, likelihood, containment owner
  • RICE-HC Maturity Model scoring per pillar (1 = Aware → 5 = Optimising)
  • Regulatory mapping: PDPA sensitive-data provisions, APHM CoP, HIPAA where cross-border data applies
  • Annual clinical-leadership + board cybersecurity simulation exercise

Vendor Alignment (draft)

  • Healthcare GRC: HITRUST MyCSF, Compliancy Group, Censinet, Clearwater
  • Clinical risk platforms: Protenus, CynergisTek — patient-data access analytics
  • Training: KnowBe4 Healthcare, HIPAA Exams — clinical-staff-specific modules
P2DRAFT

Threat Detection & Intelligence

SIEM · HC3 ADVISORIES · CLINICAL ANOMALY DETECTION

Draft — detection tuned for clinical environments, not generic enterprise IT.

Deploy SIEM with healthcare-specific threat use cases — ransomware precursors, anomalous EHR access

HITRUST 06.a

Integrate HHS Health Sector Cybersecurity Coordination Center (HC3) threat advisories into monitoring

HITRUST 07.a

Monitor for anomalous PHI access patterns — break-glass overuse, bulk record pulls

◆ RICE

Clinical-access-pattern monitoring — a generic SIEM rule set misses what looks abnormal specifically for a hospital

Deploy network detection across clinical VLANs, including medical device network segments

HITRUST 06.b

Establish 24/7 monitoring coverage or MSSP arrangement for after-hours clinical operations

◆ RICE

Clinical operations run continuously — detection coverage has to match, which most SMB hospitals underinvest in

P3DRAFT

Identity & Access Governance

ROLE-BASED CLINICAL ACCESS · BREAK-GLASS · NON-HUMAN IDENTITY

Draft — access governance has to accommodate emergency clinical workflows without becoming the excuse for over-permissioning.

Implement access control aligned to clinical role — physician, nurse, admin — not generic RBAC tiers

HIPAA §164.312(a)

Deploy break-glass emergency access with mandatory post-access review within 24 hours

◆ RICE

Break-glass has to exist for patient safety — the gap in most deployments is nobody reviews what it was used for afterward

Enforce MFA for all remote and privileged access, including shared clinical workstations where feasible

HIPAA §164.312(d)

Automate access de-provisioning tied to the credentialing system for clinical staff turnover

HITRUST 05.j

Govern non-human identities — device certificates, EHR service accounts, HL7/FHIR integration engines

◆ RICE

Non-human identity governance — the fastest-growing, least-audited access category in any modern hospital IT estate

P4DRAFT

Incident Response & Resilience

CLINICAL CONTINUITY · RANSOMWARE PLAYBOOK · PATIENT-SAFETY RTO

Draft — incident response has to protect patient care continuity first, system uptime second.

Establish a clinical-continuity incident response plan with defined downtime procedures for critical care areas

HITRUST 09.a

Define a ransomware-specific playbook with offline/paper-based fallback for patient care continuity

◆ RICE

Paper-fallback readiness — the difference between a hospital that keeps treating patients during an outage and one that can't

Meet HIPAA breach notification timelines with an internal escalation clock faster than the regulatory deadline

HIPAA §164.408

Conduct joint clinical-and-IT tabletop exercises annually — not an IT-only drill

Define RTO/RPO per system tier, weighted by patient-safety criticality rather than business criticality alone

◆ RICE

Patient-safety-weighted recovery objectives — an EHR outage and a payroll-system outage are not equivalent, and RTO targets should say so

P5DRAFT

Data Security & Privacy

PHI ENCRYPTION · CROSS-BORDER TRANSFER · DE-IDENTIFICATION

Draft — PHI carries lifetime sensitivity a financial record doesn't; controls are built around that asymmetry.

Encrypt PHI at rest and in transit across all systems, including legacy medical devices where feasible

HIPAA §164.312(e)

Implement DLP tuned for PHI patterns — MRNs, diagnosis codes — not generic PII signatures

HITRUST 09.m

Map cross-border health data transfers — cloud EHR, telemedicine — against PDPA transfer restrictions

◆ RICE

Cross-border health-data mapping — a routine cloud-EHR migration can silently become a PDPA transfer violation

De-identify data used for research and analytics per HIPAA Safe Harbor or Expert Determination method

HIPAA §164.514

Maintain a data retention and destruction schedule aligned to medical-record retention law, not generic IT policy

P6DRAFT

Supply Chain & Third-Party Risk

BAA · MDS2 · MEDICAL DEVICE SBOM

Draft — every connected device and every data-touching vendor is a clinical risk, not just a procurement line item.

Require signed Business Associate Agreements for all vendors touching PHI, with defined breach liability terms

HIPAA §164.502(e)

Collect a Manufacturer Disclosure Statement for Medical Device Security (MDS2) for every connected device

◆ RICE

MDS2 collection as a standing procurement control — most hospitals only ask for this after an incident, not before purchase

Tier vendor risk by PHI access depth and clinical-system criticality

Verify NACSA licensing status for cybersecurity service providers before and during engagement

◆ RICE

Mirrors RICE-FSI P6.09 — Cybersecurity Act 2024 licensing liability applies to healthcare NCII entities the same way it applies to banks

Establish an SBOM requirement for EHR and medical device software from key vendors

P7DRAFT

Infrastructure Best Practices

CLINICAL/IT SEGMENTATION · IEC 80001-1 · LEGACY DEVICE HARDENING

Draft — infrastructure controls account for devices that can't always be patched on a normal IT cadence.

Segment the clinical and medical-device network from the general administrative IT network

Apply the IEC 80001-1 risk management process for any new medical-device network integration

IEC 80001-1

Maintain a patch management program that accounts for FDA-validated device patch windows

◆ RICE

Device patch-window accommodation — a medical device often cannot be patched the moment a CVE drops; the program has to say what happens in the gap

Harden legacy medical devices that cannot be patched via compensating network controls

Define certificate lifecycle management for device-to-EHR integrations

P8DRAFT

Secure Application Ecosystem

PATIENT PORTAL · TELEMEDICINE · HL7/FHIR SECURITY

Draft — the application layer now includes patient-facing software, not just internal clinical systems.

Apply secure SDLC to in-house-developed clinical and patient-facing applications

Harden patient portal authentication against account-recovery abuse and credential stuffing

Secure telemedicine platform sessions end-to-end and verify vendor security posture before adoption

Test HL7/FHIR integration endpoints for injection and authorization flaws

◆ RICE

HL7/FHIR-specific testing — these interoperability standards move PHI between systems constantly and are rarely covered by generic web app scanning

Conduct API security testing for any EHR-exposed API surface

P9DRAFT

Emerging Threat Horizon

AI DIAGNOSTICS · IOMT · POST-QUANTUM · FDA SPDF

Draft — the frontier for healthcare is connected devices and clinical AI, not just networked computers.

Adopt FDA Secure Product Development Framework practices for any custom medical device software

FDA SPDF

Assess AI-diagnostic tools for adversarial-input and data-poisoning risk before clinical deployment

◆ RICE

Clinical-AI-specific risk assessment — a poisoned diagnostic model is a patient-safety incident, not just a data-science quality problem

Begin post-quantum cryptography migration planning for long-lived genomic and medical record data

◆ RICE

Genomic and medical records outlive most encryption's useful life — "harvest now, decrypt later" risk is real for 50-year-relevant health data

Monitor IoMT/OT convergence risk as connected device volume grows across the facility

Establish AI governance for clinical decision-support tools, including explainability requirements
